Welcome to The Othos Project

Othos is a voice assistant that runs on your own computer and that you can talk to. It runs on your own Windows computer, without needing to access the web unless you want to or need to for a specific task.

While this project is made initially for novice users, it can grow with you as you explore and learn the tech and usage of local AI.

Philosophy

Not the what, but the why.

AI is the "what". AI isn't going anywhere. It is an incredibly useful and powerful tool that is currently available to anyone with a device that can run it or connect to the internet. But it is a tool and like any tool, the intent of its use is in the hands of the user, not the tool itself.

So why local AI? Most people answer that with "cost" and stop there.

The Othos Project has a different answer. Yes, cost is always a factor but for us, local-only is the only ethical choice. And that makes a difference.

The software development world has an old saying "Garbage in, garbage out". It means that whatever goes into a program, be it code or even the idea behind the code, sets the tone of the final product. It applies to almost anything, really, but it is possibly the most important thing to remember when coding. The idea behind the code... the reason decides our final product so we begin as we mean to continue.

And that is important. The right choice of reason leads to a pattern of making the right decisions. LLMs use that first reason to weigh any decision in code, and you should use it to weigh your decisions too.

If cost is our first answer, we make decisions based on that. We cut corners to keep costs low, and we make decisions that apply to our costs in the moment first, based on finances, space and distance between the process and its results.

We disregard the effects of these decisions because those effects cost, too.

Ethics leads us to make cost the secondary reason, and it leads to the same final local AI. Ethics hopefully helps us keep from paying the ultimate cost. Extinction.

The best analogy to use here is the ethics of modern food supply.

Think of where our food comes from... usually big agriculture and its factory farming practices. Those practices largely grew from the fact that as towns and cities expanded, a person's ability to grow their own food became impossible. There was no land, and the cost of acquiring it was too high.

Enter factory farming with its cheaper costs and larger output. It flourishes because humans excel at out-of-sight, out-of-mind thinking and disassociation. We know intellectually the costs of factory farms to the environment and well-being of the animals involved. But since we are so far removed from it, we mostly can ignore those costs.

More and more today though, people are trying to go back to smaller farms, such as family and hobby farms that are more considerate of the earth and its creatures.

AI has almost exactly the same dilemma. Huge data centers cost our environment water and fuel to such great amounts that it puts us at risk. And the decision to ignore those costs, and to continue with that model, to make the detrimental choice, is the foundational choice those AI centers are built on and use in every decision. COST is the important part, consequences be damned. Even if the consequence is human extinction or the end of our planet.

Local AI is the hobby farm equivalent and, unlike farming, we not only have the space to do it, but we have already paid the cost of the tools and "land" to do it.

Almost everyone owns a computer... our "hobby farm". And while a single user, even an experienced heavy user, is only a grain of sand to a data center's usage, as with all sand, the grains add up. Millions of users making the ethical choice are less costly to the planet.

And ideally, that user, YOU, will hopefully make the ethical choices again and again.

Garbage in, garbage out.

What it can do

  • Chat, with a dry, friendly wit.
  • Remember notes you ask it to keep, and find them again later ("What did I tell you about my sister?").
  • Look through your webcam, but only when you ask it to.
  • Read the news, give the weather and search the web, trying free sources first.
  • Hand coding jobs to The Smithy, a background helper that writes code for you to read. It never applies, runs or installs anything itself.

What you need before you start

You needWhat it is, and why
A Windows 10 or 11 PCOthos only works on Windows for now.
About 16 GB of memory (RAM)The AI models are big. Any 16 GB PC works in the usual setup, even without a powerful graphics card.
Around 6 GB of free disk spaceFor the AI models the installer downloads, plus a little more for the programs.
An internet connection for the installSetup downloads programs and models. After that, most of Othos works on your PC.
Three free programs: Python 3.12, Node.js, OllamaBehind-the-scenes tools Othos is built on. The Install page says where to get each one and which version.
Two "keys" for the usual setupA key is like a password that lets Othos use an online service on your account. One service (Deepgram) turns your voice into text, and the other (Rime) gives Othos its speaking voice. Setup tells you where to sign up. Check each service's own page for what it costs.

Two ways to run it. The usual way is called mixed: your speech goes through those two online services, and everything else stays on your PC. The other way is fully offline: speech is handled on your PC too, but you need a graphics card, and it is for people who are comfortable setting up extra programs. Start with mixed. Advanced covers offline.
