Welcome to Othos

Othos is an assistant you talk to out loud. It runs on your own Windows computer, so you can use it without being an expert in anything. This guide assumes you have never used it, or anything like it, before.

What is it?

Think of a helpful voice in your computer. You open a web page on your own PC, press Start call, and talk. Othos listens and answers out loud. Most of its "thinking" is done by AI models (programs that have learned to understand and write language) that run on your PC instead of on someone else's servers.

If a word here is new to you, the Words explained page has a plain-language meaning for each one.

What it can do

  • Chat, with a dry, friendly wit.
  • Remember notes you ask it to keep, and find them again later ("What did I tell you about my sister?").
  • Look through your webcam, but only when you ask it to.
  • Read the news, give the weather and search the web, trying free sources first.
  • Hand coding jobs to The Smithy, a background helper that writes code for you to read. It never applies, runs or installs anything itself.

What you need before you start

You needWhat it is, and why
A Windows 10 or 11 PCOthos only works on Windows for now.
About 16 GB of memory (RAM)The AI models are big. Any 16 GB PC works in the usual setup, even without a powerful graphics card.
Around 6 GB of free disk spaceFor the AI models the installer downloads, plus a little more for the programs.
An internet connection for the installSetup downloads programs and models. After that, most of Othos works on your PC.
Three free programs: Python 3.12, Node.js, OllamaBehind-the-scenes tools Othos is built on. The Install page says where to get each one and which version.
Two "keys" for the usual setupA key is like a password that lets Othos use an online service on your account. One service (Deepgram) turns your voice into text, and the other (Rime) gives Othos its speaking voice. Setup tells you where to sign up. Check each service's own page for what it costs.

Two ways to run it. The usual way is called mixed: your speech goes through those two online services, and everything else stays on your PC. The other way is fully offline: speech is handled on your PC too, but you need a graphics card, and it is for people who are comfortable setting up extra programs. Start with mixed. Advanced covers offline.
